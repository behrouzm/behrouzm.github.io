Projects & Theses

MSc Thesis Transformer Architecture for Epigenetic Age Prediction
+

Epigenetic ageing clocks, such as the 353 CpG Horvath clock, are robust biomarkers of ageing, but unstable to the loss of CpG measurements, a flaw that is common in methylation datasets. The Transformer model shows promising performances when it comes to capturing the nonlinearity in biological data; combined with augmentation techniques, they have the potential to develop a model that can show robustness to missing values.

A 353 CpG features of Horvath clock were chosen with datasets that were split into five folds by round-robin allocation, and three different architectures — a feed-forward neural network, a TabTransformer, and an FTTransformer — were trained in two different ways: with conventional training on observed data and with augmentation. They were then tested on normal datasets and artificial randomly masked datasets with 5%–50% missingness.

All neural models showed reduction in error in fully observed data with a mean absolute error of around 3.7 years versus 7.1 years with the original clock. All neural models showed better robustness under conditions of augmentation, with a mean absolute error of 7.6 years at 50% missing data versus 16.6 years without augmentation.

These results suggest that augmentation can incorporate missingness robustness directly into methylation clocks, therefore avoiding the reliance on external preprocessing and imputation steps.

MSc Thesis FAL1 & NBAT1 lncRNAs as Cancer Stem Cell Biomarkers
+

Brain tumors and leukemia are some of the most common causes of death among children. Today, with better knowledge of cancer stem cells — recognized as a main cause of tumorigenesis — the study of response to treatment and the recurrence of cancer after treatment has become increasingly significant. One of the main reasons cancer stem cells are considered an important factor in tumorigenesis is their ability to self-renew and to create cells from other parts of the tumor.

Cancer in children can also be caused by aberrance in neurogenesis during the progression of the central nervous system, which itself can give rise to cancerous stem cells. In recent years, the role of non-coding RNAs in cellular functions has been determined, although many self-renewal regulatory pathways in these cells remain unknown. With the discovery of lncRNAs, a higher level of complexity in the regulation of in-cell activities has been found, revealing them as among the most important non-coding RNAs in these aspects.

The main goal of this study was to examine FAL1 lncRNA transcription together with the BMI1 gene in cancer stem cells' self-renewing pathways, and to study NBAT1 lncRNA transcription in the progression pathway of the central nervous system — determining their role as biomarkers with pre-determining worth for cancer risk.

This was done using Realtime PCR, a highly accurate technique, which concluded that FAL1 and BMI1 genes show increased transcription in brain tumors, while NBAT1 shows a decrease. It was also confirmed that all chosen genes possess biomarker properties, with NBAT1 showing the highest value as a biomarker.

Courses

Algorithm and Data Structure +

This course provides a comprehensive exploration of algorithmic theory and data structures, spanning from fundamental computational principles to specialized applications in computational biology. The curriculum begins with a rigorous grounding in algorithmic complexity analysis, asymptotic notation, and various sorting techniques, ranging from comparison-based methods to linear-time algorithms.

It transitions from elementary data structures like stacks and queues into advanced structures such as hash tables, graphs, and suffix-based trees. Core algorithmic paradigms — including divide-and-conquer, dynamic programming, and greedy algorithms — are examined alongside strategies for NP-hard problems like approximation and heuristics.

The second half of the course focuses on bioinformatics-specific challenges, covering exact and approximate string matching, global and local sequence alignment, the Burrows-Wheeler transform, and sequence assembly through overlap graphs. Concepts are reinforced through a dual approach of theoretical pseudocode and practical Python implementations.

Laboratory of Bioinformatics 1 & 2 +

Lab 1: This course provides a comprehensive theoretical and practical exploration of protein bioinformatics, spanning from sequence analysis to structural modeling and functional annotation. The curriculum examines the evolution of the protein universe through the lens of Next Generation Sequencing, protein domains, and databases such as UniProtKB, SCOP, and CATH. Technical instruction covers essential alignment methodologies, including local and global methods, BLAST searches, and extreme value statistics, alongside the historical and mathematical foundations of secondary structure prediction.

Significant emphasis is placed on machine learning applications, including the training and critical evaluation of neural networks and Hidden Markov Models (HMM) for biosequence analysis. Students engage in hands-on laboratories focused on structural biology, utilizing industry-standard tools like Modeller, Swiss-Model, and HMMER to build, validate, and compare 3D protein models and functional domains.

Lab 2: This course integrates bioinformatics theory with intensive hands-on programming through a comprehensive class project focused on the detection of signal peptides in protein sequences. The curriculum bridges traditional and modern computational methods by challenging students to develop original machine learning models and rigorously compare their performance against established non-ML algorithmic benchmarks. Complementing this practical application, the second half of the course explores the theoretical foundations of deep learning, providing a detailed technical overview of various neural network architectures and the underlying principles governing their design and optimization.

Introduction to Big Data Infrastructure 1 & 2 +

This course provides an end-to-end examination of modern data center architectures and the evolution of cloud computing paradigms for high-performance scientific applications. The curriculum transitions from the physical building blocks of data centers — including CPU farms, diverse storage hierarchies (DAS, NAS, SAN, and Parallel FS), and high-speed networking — to advanced Infrastructure-as-a-Service (IaaS) and Platform-as-a-Service (PaaS) models.

Technical topics include the distinction between High-Throughput (HTC) and High-Performance Computing (HPC), containerization using Docker and Kubernetes, and the emerging "computing continuum" spanning Edge, Fog, and Exascale technologies. Significant emphasis is placed on cloud automation and DevOps, covering "Infrastructure as Code," microservices, and robust authentication frameworks like OAuth and OpenID-Connect.

Practical laboratory components utilize AWS and Hadoop for MapReduce exercises, template-based orchestration, and the deployment of scalable computing models to solve real-world Big Data challenges.

Applied Genetics +

The goal is to understand the experimental designs and tools applied in genetics and genomics and approaches for genetic and genomic data analysis, with an emphasis on vertebrate genomes including genetic mapping, QTL, eQTL analysis, and next generation sequencing.

In particular, the student is able to: understand the structure of genetic variability and its phenotypic effects, browse vertebrate genomes, apply methods to identify disease genes and QTL, use software for genomic data analysis, correctly interpret results and plan genetic studies in a proper way.

DNA & RNA Dynamics +

This course provides an intensive exploration of high-throughput genomic technologies and the statistical frameworks required for their analysis. The curriculum covers the dynamics of DNA and RNA through a detailed study of microarray methodologies — including competitive, non-competitive, and BeadChip technologies — and transitions into advanced Next Generation Sequencing concepts such as single-cell RNA-sequencing and DNA methylation arrays.

The theoretical component is supported by a rigorous foundation in biostatistics, encompassing normalization methods, parametric and non-parametric testing, and multiple test correction techniques like FDR and Benjamini-Hochberg. Extensive practical training is conducted within the R and Bioconductor environments, focusing on data mining, unsupervised analysis, and the reconstruction of biological pathways using GeneOntology.

The course concludes with the hands-on application of bioinformatic tools to manage genome-wide data and analyze single-cell transcriptomics.

Computational Methods for Bioinformatics +

This course integrates fundamental computer science with bioinformatics applications, focusing on the development of custom tools for the sequence and structure analysis of biomolecules. The first module provides a rigorous immersion into Python programming, covering core concepts such as data structures, recursion, and object-oriented programming.

Building on this technical foundation, the second module introduces the Linux environment and the theoretical frameworks of dynamic programming and pairwise alignment algorithms. The curriculum culminates in the practical implementation of local, global, and semiglobal alignment heuristics, bridging the gap between software engineering and genomic annotation.

Biomedical Databases +

This course offers a detailed examination of database architecture and information management within the biomedical sciences, split between specialized biological repositories and general database systems. The curriculum introduces major biomedical platforms — including PubMed, UniProt, PDB, GenBank, and Ensembl — focusing on efficient information retrieval and data navigation.

It further explores the technical foundations of data storage, covering the structures and features of relational and non-relational systems, such as key-value and graph databases. Practical components emphasize the streaming of biological data, programmatic interaction for automated retrieval and annotation, and the end-to-end process of designing and creating custom database solutions.
